## Design Context

### Users
VGCenter serves competitive Pokemon VGC players broadly: tournament grinders, ladder players, and dedicated teambuilders. They use the calculator during meta analysis and match prep, while optimizing team compositions, and when pressure-testing ideas during theorycrafting.

The interface should help them reach answers quickly without feeling noisy or over-explained. It should support focused comparison, scenario validation, and faster decision-making before events or during iterative team work.

### Brand Personality
Preparation. Understandable. Concise.

The product should feel confident and focused rather than flashy for its own sake. It should improve the player's clarity about the current meta, their own team composition, and the decisions they need to make next.

### Aesthetic Direction
Retain the current dark competitive-arena foundation, but refine it into a more disciplined tournament console. The visual language should feel tactical, legible, and composed, with reduced gradient intensity and fewer distracting effects competing for attention.

Reference direction:
- The utility and tone of `https://nerd-of-now.github.io/` are useful.
- Avoid the feeling of too many controls competing for attention at once.

This should remain unmistakably performance-oriented, but the atmosphere needs to serve comprehension first. Accent colors should mark priority and state, not flood the screen. Layouts should privilege scanning, grouping, and quick comparison over spectacle.

### Design Principles
1. Lead with decision clarity. Every screen should make matchup analysis, prep, and theorycrafting easier to read at a glance.
2. Keep visual intensity under control. Use restrained color grading, sparse accents, and fewer competing gradients so emphasis stays meaningful.
3. Make complex inputs feel organized. Group related controls tightly, reveal secondary complexity progressively, and avoid presenting every action with equal weight.
4. Design for competitive flow. Assume repeated use by serious players who want speed, confidence, and minimal friction while comparing scenarios.
5. Preserve a distinctive arena identity without sacrificing readability. The interface can feel tactical and high-performance, but it should never become distracting.
